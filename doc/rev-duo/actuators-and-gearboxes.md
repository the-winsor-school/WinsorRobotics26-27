# REV DUO Actuators & Gearboxes Guide

> **Winsor Robotics FTC Resource Directory**  
> Component: DC Motors, Servos, and the UltraPlanetary Modular Gearbox

Actuators are the muscles of the robot. Selecting the wrong actuator or gear ratio will cause mechanisms to either stall, overheat, or move too slowly to be competitive. This guide outlines the specifications, gearing calculations, and assembly rules for the REV DUO motor and servo family.

---

## 1. DC Motor Comparison

REV offers two primary DC motors for the DUO build system: the **HD Hex Motor** and the **Core Hex Motor**.

| Specification | HD Hex Motor ([REV-41-1291](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-41-1291/)) | Core Hex Motor ([REV-41-1300](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-41-1300/)) |
|---|---|---|
| **Motor Type** | 550-class brushed DC motor | Compact planetary gearmotor |
| **Operating Voltage** | 12V DC | 12V DC |
| **Base Ungeared Speed** | 6,000 RPM | N/A (permanently geared) |
| **Geared Output Speed** | 100 to 2,000+ RPM (via UltraPlanetary) | 125 RPM (fixed 20:1 internal gearbox) |
| **Stall Torque** | High (depends on UltraPlanetary ratio) | 3.2 N·m (453 oz-in) |
| **Output Interface** | Female 5mm hex socket (with UP gearbox) | Female 5mm hex socket (passes through) |
| **Encoder Type** | Integrated Quad Hall Effect (motor shaft) | Integrated Quad Hall Effect (output shaft) |
| **Encoder Counts** | **28 ticks/rev** of motor shaft | **288 ticks/rev** of output shaft |
| **Best Application** | Drivetrains, heavy lift elevators, rotating arms | Intakes, indexers, wrist joints, light mechanisms |

### When to choose HD Hex:
Always choose HD Hex for primary robot subsystems requiring high mechanical power or variable speed:
- Drivetrains (Mecanum, Tank, Omni)
- Multi-stage linear slides / cascade lifts
- Flywheel shooters and high-speed launchers
- High-load rotating arms and shoulder pivots

### When to choose Core Hex:
Choose Core Hex when space and weight are strictly limited and modest torque/speed is sufficient:
- Roller intakes and game piece collectors
- Funnel and feeder belts
- Light wrist pivots
- *Note:* Because the 5mm hex bore passes entirely through the Core Hex motor body, you can mount a shaft that extends out of both sides simultaneously!

## 2. UltraPlanetary Gearbox System ([REV-41-1600](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-41-1600/))

The UltraPlanetary is REV's modular cartridge gearbox designed specifically for the HD Hex Motor. It allows builders to stack 3:1, 4:1, and 5:1 planetary cartridges to achieve custom gear ratios.

```
 [HD Hex Motor] -> [Stage 1 Cartridge] -> [Stage 2 Cartridge] -> [Stage 3 Cartridge] -> [Output Stage]
```

### Cartridge Ratio Reference
Cartridges are labeled with nominal ratios for simplicity, but when writing autonomous distance algorithms, use **exact ratios**:

| Product Code | Nominal Ratio | Tooth Count | Exact Ratio | Motor Encoder Ticks/Output Rev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **REV-41-1601** | **3:1** | 84:29 | **2.896 : 1** | ~81.1 ticks |
| **REV-41-1602** | **4:1** | 76:21 | **3.619 : 1** | ~101.3 ticks |
| **REV-41-1603** | **5:1** | 68:13 | **5.230 : 1** | ~146.5 ticks |

---

### The Golden Rule of UltraPlanetary Stacking

> [!CAUTION]
> **ALWAYS PLACE THE HIGHEST GEAR REDUCTION CLOSEST TO THE MOTOR!**  
> *Correct:* Motor $\rightarrow$ 5:1 $\rightarrow$ 4:1 $\rightarrow$ Output  
> *Wrong:* Motor $\rightarrow$ 4:1 $\rightarrow$ 5:1 $\rightarrow$ Output

**Why?**  
Torque multiplies across each stage. The output stage of the gearbox experiences the greatest torque. 
- A 5:1 cartridge has a smaller sun gear and smaller planet teeth than a 3:1 or 4:1 cartridge.
- If a 5:1 cartridge is placed at the output, the massive torque generated by preceding stages will shear the small teeth off the 5:1 planet gears during a motor stall.
- By placing the 5:1 cartridge against the motor (where torque is lowest), and the 4:1 or 3:1 cartridge at the output (where teeth are beefiest), the gearbox can withstand maximum load.

---

### Common Gearbox Configurations & Specs

| Stack Configuration | Exact Ratio | Free Speed (RPM) | Output Stall Torque | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3:1** | 2.89 : 1 | 2,076 RPM | ~0.30 N·m | High-speed flywheel launch rollers |
| **4:1** | 3.61 : 1 | 1,662 RPM | ~0.38 N·m | High-speed feeder wheels |
| **5:1** | 5.23 : 1 | 1,147 RPM | ~0.55 N·m | Rapid intake mechanisms |
| **4:1 + 3:1** | 10.49 : 1 | 572 RPM | ~1.10 N·m | Agility drivetrains (lightweight robots) |
| **5:1 + 3:1** | 15.15 : 1 | 396 RPM | ~1.59 N·m | Balanced drivetrains |
| **5:1 + 4:1** | **18.88 : 1** | **318 RPM** | **~1.98 N·m** | **FTC Competition Standard Drivetrain** |
| **5:1 + 5:1** | 27.35 : 1 | 219 RPM | ~2.87 N·m | Fast single-stage lifts, hanging winches |
| **5:1 + 4:1 + 3:1** | 54.68 : 1 | 110 RPM | ~5.74 N·m | High-load multi-stage cascading lifts |
| **5:1 + 5:1 + 4:1** | 98.74 : 1 | 61 RPM | ~10.36 N·m | Heavy robot lifting arms |

### Load Limits & Safety Warning
- The UltraPlanetary gearbox is rated for an absolute maximum torque of **40 N·m**.
- While 4-stage gearboxes are physically possible, stacking 4 stages can easily generate enough stall torque to destroy the output cartridge housing. Winsor Robotics robots should rarely exceed **3 stages**.
- Always support the output shaft with an external bearing if there is side-load (e.g., from chain or cantilevered wheels).

### Ultra 90 Degree Gearbox ([REV-41-1615](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-41-1615/))
- A 1:1 spiral bevel gear attachment that bolts directly to an UltraPlanetary cartridge stack.
- Allows mounting the HD Hex motor flat along the robot chassis rail, eliminating motor overhang in tight 18" FTC sizing cubes.

## 3. Smart Robot Servo (SRS)

The **Smart Robot Servo (SRS)** ([REV-41-1097](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-41-1097/)) is a configurable high-torque actuator featuring metal gears and a standard 25T spline.

### SRS Specifications
- **Operating Voltage:** 4.8V – 6.0V
- **Stall Torque (at 6V):** 13.5 kg·cm (187.5 oz-in / 1.32 N·m)
- **Speed (at 6V):** 0.14 sec / 60°
- **Spline Standard:** 25-Tooth (compatible with REV metal servo horns and servo brackets)

### The Two Operating Modes

| Mode | Behavior | TeamCode Java Type | Typical Mechanisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Angular Mode** | Moves to a specific angle (up to 270°) and holds position | `Servo` (`setPosition(0.0 to 1.0)`) | Claws, gates, wrist angle, camera tilt, latches |
| **Continuous Rotation Mode** | Rotates continuously in either direction at variable speed | `CRServo` (`setPower(-1.0 to 1.0)`) | Turrets, intake rollers, lead screws |

### SRS Programmer ([REV-31-1108](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-31-1108/))
The SRS Programmer is a handheld tool used to switch modes and set custom angular limits on the servo:
1. Plug the servo into the 3-pin port on the programmer.
2. Slide the mode switch between **Continuous** and **Standard**.
3. Press and hold the **PROGRAM** button until the LED confirms the change.
4. *Angular Limiting:* You can adjust physical travel limits (e.g. restricting travel between 30° and 120°) directly on the programmer without needing to code software stops.

## 4. Servo Power Module & Electrical Protection

> [!WARNING]
> **THE 5V SERVO BROWNOUT RISK:**  
> The REV Control Hub provides internal 5V power to its 6 servo ports, but the total current is limited to approximately **2.0 Amps aggregate**. A single Smart Robot Servo stalling under load can draw 1.8A to 2.0A! Running multiple servos under mechanical resistance will drop the 5V bus, causing the Control Hub to brown out and restart in the middle of a match.

### Prevention Strategies:
1. **Never stall a servo against a hard stop:** In Angular Mode, ensure your target positions (`setPosition`) stop 5–10% before hitting physical aluminum structure.
2. **Use the Servo Power Module (SPM) ([REV-11-1144](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-11-1144/)):**
   - Plugs into the 12V battery power bus (via XT30).
   - Supplies up to **6V @ 15A continuous** to up to 6 servos simultaneously.
   - Eliminates all servo load from the Control Hub's internal regulator.

## 5. Software Integration with TeamCode

When you add a motor or servo to your robot, wrap it in our team's `MechComponent` object model:

```java
// Example: Wrapping an UltraPlanetary HD Hex motor in our SpinnyIntake component
intake = new SpinnyIntake(hardwareMap, "intakeMotor", (motor, gamepad) -> {
    if (gamepad.right_trigger > 0.1) {
        motor.setPower(gamepad.right_trigger);
    } else if (gamepad.left_trigger > 0.1) {
        motor.setPower(-gamepad.left_trigger);
    } else {
        motor.setPower(0.0);
    }
});
```

See [02-ftc-hardware-toolbox.md](../FlintLessons/GettingStarted/02-ftc-hardware-toolbox.md) for full examples of `DcMotor`, `Servo`, and `CRServo` wrappers.
